Our story

Amlak means
properties.

أملاك — the Arabic word for properties. Not the paperwork around them, and not the software that manages them: the buildings themselves. Which is roughly the whole idea — you own buildings, and the software should keep them straight so you can go and do something else.

Why it exists

Built by a landlord, not for a landlord.

Amlak was written by someone managing triple-net retail out of a spreadsheet, to solve the problems that spreadsheet kept causing. Every feature in it started as something that had already gone wrong once.

01

A file called CAM_2023_FINAL_v4.xlsx

Every lease abstracted by hand into a tab. Every tenant's share a formula pointing at a cell three sheets over. It worked, in the sense that it produced numbers.

02

A building's square footage was wrong

Correcting it took four seconds. Finding everything that had quietly depended on it — every tenant's share, every estimate, every invoice already sent, the whole year's reconciliation — took considerably longer. Nothing had warned anyone. That is the problem Amlak is actually built around: not the arithmetic, but everything downstream of it.

03

Amlak

A figure is entered once. Everything that reads it is derived, not copied — so changing the tax bill moves the shares, the invoices and the year-end settle-up together, and a year you have already closed and billed is deliberately left alone. The reconciliation that used to take a fortnight is a screen you look at.

Our approach

AI where it earns its keep, and nowhere else.

Amlak uses a model in exactly three places — all of them jobs that involve reading a document a human would otherwise have to read. It does not use one to decide what you owe.

§

Reading documents

Leases, addendums, certificates of insurance, service contracts, bank statements. Turning a PDF into terms you can check — shown next to the clause it came from.

?

Answering questions

Asking twenty leases something at once and getting back the two that say something unusual, with the section number, so you can go and read it yourself.

⚑

Flagging problems

Noticing that a lease has no CAM cap, or that two clauses in it disagree about the roof. Judgement stays yours; Amlak just makes sure you saw it.

∑

Never the money

Every dollar figure is arithmetic — shares, prorations, escalations, reconciliations — computed from the terms you confirmed. No model is asked what a tenant owes.

Where things stand

Small, private, and honest about it.

Private beta

Amlak runs real portfolios today, but there is no signup — deliberately, while the product is still being shaped by the people using it. A new account starts with a conversation about what you run and whether this actually suits it, which is as much about us saying no as it is about you saying yes. There is no pricing page because there is not yet a price to put on it.

The demo is not a mock-up or a video. It is the actual application running on a sample portfolio, and you can break it however you like — nothing in it touches real data.
