Why landlords move off the spreadsheet

One figure, entered once, everywhere it belongs.

A spreadsheet lets you change a number in one cell and quietly leaves eleven other cells wrong. Amlak's whole design is the opposite of that.

It reads the lease

Upload the PDF. Amlak pulls the term, the base rent, every escalation step, the renewal options and their notice deadlines, the CAM basis and the tenant's share of the building — and shows you the clause each one came from.

Triple-net, split correctly

Pro-rata by square footage, or the override the lease negotiated. Estimates while the year runs, actuals when it closes, and a reconciliation that says who owes and who's owed — to the cent.

Money, matched to a tenant

Drop in a bank statement. Amlak reads the deposits, proposes which tenant and which month each one settles, and shows you what it couldn't place instead of quietly guessing.

Nothing goes stale

Change a building's size, a tax bill or a rent step and every invoice, share and total that depended on it is rebuilt — except in a year you've already closed, which is left exactly as billed.

The shape of it

Four things in. Everything else derived.

Most of what you look at in Amlak is not stored anywhere — it is worked out from these four every time you open the page. That is the reason nothing goes stale, and the reason there is no spreadsheet to remember to update.

You enter

  • Lease PDFterm, rent, steps, share
  • Bank statementdeposits, dated
  • Tax billthe year’s actualreassessed in August
  • Service contractlandscaping, snow, HVAC

Amlak derives

  • Tenant invoiceissued, then frozen
  • Rent ledgertwelve months, one row each
  • CAM reconciliationwho owes, who’s owed
  • Income & expensesthe sheet your accountant wants
You enter four things. A lease, a bank statement, a tax bill, a service contract. Each one goes in once, on the screen where it belongs.
They all arrive in the same place. Amlak holds one version of each figure — the tenant's share, the year's actual cost, what has actually been paid.
Everything else is worked out from them. The invoice, the ledger, the reconciliation and the accountant's sheet are not four more things to keep up to date. They are four views of those four.
So the tax bill lands in August, and all four move. Not one of them is re-typed — except in a year you have already closed, which stays exactly as it was billed.

Lease abstraction

Read a lease in a minute, not an afternoon

Amlak reads the whole document — the original, and every addendum and rider stacked on top of it — and fills in the terms it finds. Each one is shown next to the clause it came from, so you're checking its work, not retyping the lease.

  • Term, base rent, escalations, options, CAM basis, square footage, who pays for the roof
  • Addendums and amendments applied on top, in the order they were signed
  • Anything it can't find is marked silent — never quietly filled with a guess

CAM & tax recovery

The split the lease negotiated, not the one that's easy

Every tenant's share is derived from their square footage against the building's — unless their lease says otherwise, in which case Amlak uses that instead. Charges are prorated to the days they were actually in term, so a mid-year move-in pays for the months it was there and not a day more.

  • CAM, real estate taxes and roof tracked as separate pools
  • Bill an estimate through the year, then reconcile to the actual
  • Costs you choose to absorb stay off every tenant's invoice — and still show in what they cost you

Bank statement import

Reads your statement, names the tenant

Drop in the PDF your bank gave you. Amlak reads every line, proposes which tenant and which month each deposit settles, and files the money out the other side. What it can't confidently place, it hands back — an unplaced line is visible, not invented.

  • Learns your payees, so next month's statement lands mostly pre-matched
  • Money out is filed to a CAM bucket, an expense, or marked not billed back
  • Every line you decide is recorded and reversible while it's still safe to reverse

The rent ledger

Twelve months, one row per tenant

What each tenant owed, what actually arrived and what's still outstanding — month by month, across the whole property. The grid tells you who is behind before you have to go looking, and it waits for a month to actually end before it calls anyone late.

  • A rent step that landed but isn't being paid yet is raised on its own
  • An invoice that has drifted from the schedule says so, and offers to rebuild
  • Reminders can email you — or you can leave them on the dashboard
