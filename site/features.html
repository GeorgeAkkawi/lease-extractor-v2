01 · Lease abstraction

Read a lease in a minute, not an afternoon

Upload the PDF — scanned or digital. Amlak reads the whole document and fills in the terms it finds, each one shown beside the clause it came from so you are checking its work rather than retyping the lease. Addendums and riders stack on top in the order they were signed, so the terms you end up with are the ones actually in force.

  • Term dates, base rent, escalation steps, renewal options and their notice windows
  • Square footage, CAM basis, share overrides, who carries the roof and structure
  • A term the lease never mentions is marked silent — never filled with a guess

02 · Ask your leases

Ask a question, get the clause back

Ask in plain English — of one lease, of a property, or of the whole portfolio — and Amlak answers from the documents, citing the section it read. It is a way of searching twenty leases at once for the one that says something unusual.

  • “Who pays for the parking lot at Sunrise Plaza?”
  • “Which of my tenants can assign without my consent?”
  • Every answer names the lease and the section — check it in one click
Which leases let the tenant sublet without asking me?
Two of twenty-three. Corner Grocer may assign to an affiliate without consent (§14.3), and Bright Cleaners requires notice but not consent (§14.1). The other twenty-one all require your written consent.
What's the CAM cap on Corner Grocer?
5% a year, non-cumulative, on controllable expenses only — taxes and insurance are excluded from the cap (§5.6).

04 · CAM, taxes & roof

The split the lease negotiated, not the one that's easy

Each tenant's share comes from their square footage against the building's — unless their lease says otherwise, in which case Amlak uses that. Charges prorate to the days actually in term, so a mid-year move-in pays for the months it was there and not a day more. CAM, real estate taxes and roof are three separate pools, because leases treat them as three separate obligations.

  • Bill an estimate through the year, reconcile to the actual when it closes
  • A cost you decide to absorb stays off every invoice — and still shows in what the year cost you
  • Close a year and it is frozen: later edits elsewhere can't move a bill you've already sent

05 · Escalations & options

Rent steps that raise themselves, options that chase you

The steps Amlak read out of the lease are scheduled, and they apply on their own date — the months before a raise stay at the old rent, because they were billed at the old rent. Renewal options count down to their notice deadline and tell you before the window shuts, not after.

  • Fixed steps, percentage steps and CPI adjustments
  • An option whose notice window has passed is marked lapsed, with the reason
  • Changing a rent mid-year splits the year, rather than repricing January

06 · Bank statement import

Reads your statement, names the tenant

Drop in the PDF your bank gave you. Amlak reads every line, proposes which tenant and which month each deposit settles, and files the money out the other side. What it can't confidently place, it hands back to you — an unplaced line stays visible instead of being invented.

  • Learns your payees, so next month's statement lands mostly pre-matched
  • Money out is filed to a CAM bucket, an expense, or marked not billed back
  • A line you filed stays on the record, and is reversible while reversing is still safe

07 · The rent ledger

Twelve months, one row per tenant

What each tenant owed, what arrived and what is still outstanding, month by month, across the property. The grid tells you who is behind before you go looking — and it waits for a month to actually end before it calls anyone late, because the statement that would settle it doesn't exist yet.

  • A rent step that landed but isn't being paid yet is raised on its own
  • An invoice that has drifted from the schedule says so, and offers to rebuild it
  • Reminders can email you, or stay on the dashboard — your choice, per type

08 · E-signature

Sign it without a second subscription

Send a lease, an extension or an amendment straight from the record it belongs to. The tenant signs in their browser with no account. You countersign, and Amlak builds the executed PDF with a certificate of completion — who signed, when, and from where.

  • No DocuSign bill, and nothing to reconcile between two systems
  • Sending and countersigning move no money — the figures change only when you apply them
  • Service contracts sign the same way, through the same audit trail

10 · What actually stayed

NOI, and the money NOI has never known about

Net operating income is struck before your tenants pay any of it back, so on a triple-net property it understates what you kept — by exactly the reimbursement. Amlak quotes NOI unchanged, then carries beside it everything it doesn't include: what tenants reimbursed, what you absorbed, other income, and what you drew out.

  • A distribution is not an expense — it reduces your equity, and is shown that way
  • Income and expenses export to a workbook your accountant can open
  • Every figure on the sheet is derived from the same functions the screens use
