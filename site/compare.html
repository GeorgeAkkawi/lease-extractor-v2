The other half of an honest comparison

What Amlak doesn't do.

If one of these is how you run your business, the enterprise platforms are a better fit today, and we would rather you knew that now than after a demo call.

Not built, and not pretending to be

  • No accounting integration. Amlak doesn't sync with QuickBooks, Xero or Bill.com. It reads your bank statement instead — which covers the money, but it is not your general ledger and doesn't try to be.
  • No tenant portal. Your tenants don't log in. They receive emails, invoices and documents to sign; there is nowhere for them to go and look things up themselves.
  • No work orders or maintenance tickets. Nothing for logging a repair, dispatching a vendor, or tracking it to completion.
  • No online rent collection. Amlak records what arrived; it doesn't take the payment.
  • No budgeting module. You can see what a year cost and export it, but there is no budget-versus-actual to plan against.
  • Not built for residential or for large portfolios. It is aimed squarely at a landlord with a handful of NNN retail properties. Hundreds of units is somebody else's product.

Amlak is in private beta, and accounts are issued by request rather than by signup.
